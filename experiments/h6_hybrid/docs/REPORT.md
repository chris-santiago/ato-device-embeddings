# H6 Hybrid Experiment — Report

> Results document. Written after experiment completion.
> Pre-registration: HYPOTHESIS.md. Metrics artifact: figures/h6_metrics.json.

---

## Abstract

H6 tests whether a two-stage embedding scorer (FastText skip-gram mean-pool + set-membership gate) achieves ROC-AUC greater than the trivial set-membership baseline on k=1 country-change spoof attacks, using a hybrid dataset whose login events are chain-sampled from real RBA marginals. The primary finding is **CONFIRMED**: two-stage embedding beats trivial at k=1 with non-overlapping 95% bootstrap CIs (two-stage ROC=0.980 vs trivial ROC=0.943, Δ=+0.037). More decisively, under a realistic 1:100 attack-to-benign ratio, **PR-AUC** reveals the operational gap: mp_raw PR=0.892 vs trivial PR=0.104 — an 8.6× lift. Rank-normalization degrades PR-AUC dramatically (PR=0.215) while preserving ROC-AUC, making raw cosine distance the operationally preferred scorer. A fleet attack diagnostic with a temporal cross-account blocklist model (lag=10d, window=30d) finds: (1) trivial/two_stage remain blind during the 39-account cold-start window; (2) `two_stage_blocklist` cleanly catches all 62 post-lag fleet accounts (310 TP, 60.8% prec); (3) the `combined` scorer (blocklist + mp_raw) achieves higher ROC-AUC (0.998) but lower PR-AUC (0.771) than mp_raw alone on the full fleet population (PR-AUC 0.918) — forcing post-lag events to score=1.0 disrupts the natural cosine-distance ranking, a ranking artifact that penalizes PR-AUC while inflating ROC-AUC; on the pre-lag cold-start population only (fleet residual, n=39 accounts), mp_raw achieves PR-AUC 0.948. The **final deployment recommendation** is a two-layer composite: system-level blocklist (upstream deny-list) + single-stage mp_raw with no per-account gate. The pre-registered hypothesis — that two-stage beats trivial at k=1 — is confirmed, but hypothesis confirmation is not deployment endorsement: single-stage mp_raw dominates two-stage on every metric, and the per-account allow-list gate actively blinds the model during the cold-start fleet window.

## 1. Context and Motivation

### 1.1 Prior Findings

H5 stress-tested H2 mean-pool FastText (i.i.d. synthetic, 30-token vocabulary) across k=1/2/3 spoof levels. Key outcomes:

| Finding | Value |
|---------|-------|
| k=1 two-stage ROC-AUC | 0.530 — decisively below trivial 0.750 |
| k=3 two-stage ROC-AUC | 0.848 — beats trivial |
| Two-stage gate lift at k=1 | ≈+0.01 (negligible) |

The open question: is k=1 failure a property of the toy feature space (30 tokens, no co-occurrence structure) or a fundamental limit of the mean-pool embedding approach?

### 1.2 Hypothesis

Using RBA-calibrated marginals — with chain-sampled co-occurrence structure, 230 unique tokens, and realistic feature distributions — will produce a richer embedding space where the two-stage gate architecture lifts above trivial on k=1 country-change spoofing.

### 1.3 Design Changes from H5

1. **Vocabulary depth**: RBA chain-sampling yields 229 unique tokens vs. 30 in H5.
2. **Class imbalance**: 1:100 attack-to-enrollment-negative ratio vs. 1:1 in H5. ROC-AUC misleads at 1:1; PR-AUC is the primary metric.
3. **Scorer variants**: Added rank-norm and two-stage-rank-norm alongside mp_raw.
4. **Attack types**: Spoof k=1/k=2/k=3, novel device, and fleet device attacks all evaluated.

## 2. Dataset and Experimental Design

**Accounts:** N=400 synthetic accounts. Each account's training corpus is generated by chain-sampling from RBA clean login marginals (11.7M rows, filtered to `login_successful=True, is_attack_ip=False, is_ato=False`).

**Feature correlation structure (chain-sampling):**
- Device: `(os, device_type)` jointly sampled; `browser` conditioned on `os`.
- Location: `country` from marginal; `region`, `asn_bucket` conditioned on `country`.
- `rtt_bucket`: sampled independently (below-chance signal per H3 ablation).

**Training split:** N_CENTROID=40 events used for centroid; N_CALIB=20 used for rank-norm calibration baseline.

**Fleet injection:** FLEET_FRAC=0.25 — a shared global device injected into 25% of accounts' training histories, simulating shared infrastructure.

**Attack types:**
- `spoof_k1`: country changed to a different country; region and ASN resampled from new country's distribution. Device features unchanged (VPN-realistic).
- `spoof_k2`: country + one additional device feature changed.
- `spoof_k3`: country + two additional device features changed.
- `novel`: fully novel device (new 6-tuple, not in training set).
- `fleet`: known shared fleet device as the attack event.

**Negatives (enrollment):** N_ENROLL_NEG=N_ACCOUNTS×100 novel enrollment events per account per attack type (1:100 imbalance).

**Scorers:**
- `mp_raw`: cosine distance from mean-pool embedding to account centroid.
- `mp_rank_norm`: CDF-rank of mp_raw against per-account calibration set.
- `trivial`: 1 if device key not in training set, 0 if known.
- `two_stage`: set-membership gate (known→0), else mp_raw.
- `two_stage_rank_norm`: set-membership gate (known→0), else mp_rank_norm.

**Evaluation:** Bootstrap ROC-AUC and PR-AUC (1,000 iterations, seed=42); point-estimate top-1% precision/recall/F1.

### 3.3 Fleet Attack Diagnostic

Fleet device attacks expose a two-regime detection problem. The experiment models a realistic temporal cross-account blocklist (BLOCKLIST_LAG=10d, ATTACK_WINDOW=30d):
- **Pre-lag (cold-start window):** First fleet attack observed at t≈0.2d; blocklist activates at t≈10.2d. Fleet attacks during this window cannot be caught by any set-membership mechanism.
- **Post-lag:** Blocklist active; confirmed fleet devices are flagged regardless of account membership.

With 101 fleet accounts (FLEET_FRAC=0.25): **39 pre-lag accounts (cold-start), 62 post-lag accounts (caught by blocklist)**.

#### Without blocklist (original scorers)

| Scorer | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Top-1% TP / prec |
|--------|---------|--------|------------------|
| **mp_raw** | **0.992** | **0.918** | **445 TP / 87.3%** |
| mp_rank_norm | 0.974 | 0.228 | 167 TP / 32.7% |
| trivial | 0.459 | 0.010 | **0 TP / 0.0%** |
| two_stage | 0.459 | 0.010 | **0 TP / 0.0%** |
| two_stage_rank_norm | 0.459 | 0.010 | **0 TP / 0.0%** |

The set-membership gate labels the fleet device as "known" → score=0 for trivial/two_stage. Only mp_raw detects fleet attacks via the continuous embedding distance signal.

#### With temporal cross-account blocklist (3 additional scorers)

| Scorer | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Top-1% TP / prec | Notes |
|--------|---------|--------|------------------|-------|
| trivial_blocklist | 0.765 | 0.046 | 42 TP / 8.2% | Post-lag caught, but ties at 1.0 with novel enrollment negs → low precision |
| **two_stage_blocklist** | **0.790** | **0.474** | **310 TP / 60.8%** | Gate isolates blocklist score=1.0 from enrollment negs → cleaner post-lag signal |
| combined (BL+mp_raw) | 0.998 | 0.771 | 415 TP / 81.4% | Post-lag via blocklist, pre-lag via mp_raw |

**Key finding:** `two_stage_blocklist` cleanly catches all 310 post-lag fleet attacks (62 accounts × 5 events) because the two-stage gate prevents enrollment negatives from tying at score=1.0 — enrollment negatives that are novel devices get sent to Stage 2 (embedding) rather than the blocklist path.

**Counterintuitive result — combined scorer:** `combined` achieves higher ROC-AUC (0.998 vs 0.992) but **lower PR-AUC (0.771 vs 0.918)** than mp_raw alone. Root cause: boosting 310 post-lag fleet events to score=1.0 disrupts the natural cosine-distance ranking that gives mp_raw high precision across all recall levels. The forced score=1.0 events consume 61% of the top-1% quota, leaving only 200 slots for pre-lag fleet and enrollment negatives to compete — some high-ranking pre-lag fleet events get pushed out. At 1:100 imbalance, PR-AUC is the honest metric; the combined scorer adds operational certainty for post-lag events but does not improve unified detection metrics over mp_raw alone.

**Architecture implication:** For full-coverage fleet detection, mp_raw alone remains the best unified scorer. `two_stage_blocklist` is the right architecture when post-lag certainty is required (hard decision) and pre-lag coverage is acceptable to miss; `combined` provides both but the PR-AUC regression relative to mp_raw means the embedding signal dominates and the blocklist adds only marginal unified value.

#### Fleet residual: model performance on cold-start window only

The `fleet` evaluation above mixes two populations that in production are handled by separate layers (blocklist vs. model). A cleaner view evaluates the model only on events that actually reach it: **pre-lag fleet accounts** (39/101), after the blocklist has filtered post-lag events upstream.

| Scorer | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Top-1% TP / prec |
|--------|---------|--------|------------------|
| **mp_raw** | **0.997** | **0.948** | **180 TP / 91.8%** |
| mp_rank_norm | 0.974 | 0.229 | 65 TP / 33.2% |
| trivial | 0.457 | 0.010 | 0 TP / 0.0% |
| two_stage | 0.457 | 0.010 | 0 TP / 0.0% |
| two_stage_rank_norm | 0.458 | 0.010 | 0 TP / 0.0% |

**Residual mp_raw PR=0.948 exceeds the full-fleet PR=0.918.** Removing post-lag events eliminates the ranking artifact introduced by the 1.0-score collapse in `combined` and shows the embedding's true capability against cold-start attacks in isolation.

**The two-layer composite:** blocklist catches 62/101 = 61% of fleet accounts with certainty (precision=1.0 by construction); mp_raw handles the residual 39/101 = 39% cold-start window at PR=0.948. These two layers are cleanly decoupled and each evaluated on the population it actually serves.

## 4. Pre-Registered Contingency

The pre-registered hypothesis expected trivial baseline ≈ 0.75. Observed trivial k=1 ROC-AUC = 0.943. The contingency clause was triggered:

> "If trivial is not approximately 0.75 (e.g., it is significantly higher because enrollment negatives are also novel in RBA feature-space), do not declare failure against a fixed threshold. Instead, restate the finding as 'embedding adds X over trivial.'"

**Restated verdict:** Two-stage embedding achieves Δ+0.037 ROC-AUC over trivial at k=1, with non-overlapping 95% CIs. **Primary criterion: CONFIRMED.**

The elevated trivial baseline (0.943 vs expected 0.75) is mechanically explained: RBA chain-sampling produces enrollment negatives that are themselves largely novel device tuples, causing trivial to score them 1.0 alongside spoofs. The 1:100 imbalance means enrollment negatives dominate — trivial assigns all of them score=1.0, which is partially correct, inflating ROC-AUC past 0.75. PR-AUC correctly penalizes this: trivial PR=0.104 at 1:100 vs mp_raw PR=0.892.

## 5. Failure Mode Analysis

### 5.1 Rank-Normalization Collapses PR-AUC

Rank-normalization (CDF transform against per-account calibration set) improves ROC-AUC slightly over trivial but collapses PR-AUC from 0.892 to 0.215 at k=1. Root cause: the CDF transform compresses the score distribution, reducing the score margin between positives and negatives. Under heavy class imbalance (1:100), the compressed margin causes the precision-recall curve to degrade sharply.

### 5.2 Two-Stage Gate Slightly Hurts mp_raw

At k=1, two_stage ROC-AUC (0.980) is slightly below mp_raw (0.995). The gate hurts because: on this dataset, known devices already receive low cosine distances from the embedding model (they were in training). Forcing known scores to exactly 0.0 creates ties, which random tie-breaking resolves adversarially during AUC computation. The gate architecture was designed for scenarios with more training-set drift; on i.i.d.-within-account data it adds no benefit.

### 5.3 Why k=1 Now Works (vs H5 Failure)

H5 i.i.d. synthetic: 30 unique tokens. Country-change spoof (analogous to H5's tz-change) moves only 1/30 of the vocabulary space — insufficient signal. H6 hybrid: 229 unique tokens from RBA marginals with realistic country/region/ASN co-occurrence structure. Country tokens span a much larger and more discriminative region of the embedding space. Vocabulary richness resolves the k=1 failure.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Metric Hierarchy Under Class Imbalance

At 1:100, ROC-AUC no longer discriminates between models: trivial achieves 0.943, mp_rank_norm achieves 0.972, mp_raw achieves 0.995. All appear competitive. PR-AUC reveals the truth: trivial=0.104, rank_norm=0.215, mp_raw=0.892. In any real deployment setting, ROC-AUC should be secondary; PR-AUC and top-k precision/recall are the decision-relevant metrics.

### 6.2 Architecture Recommendation

**The fleet_residual analysis retires the two-stage architecture as a recommendation.**

The two-stage per-account allow-list gate (known device → score=0) fails during the cold-start window precisely because the fleet device is in `known_devices` — it appeared as a normal login before the attack. The gate fires and scores it 0. Once the blocklist activates post-lag, the event never reaches the model anyway, making the gate irrelevant. The gate only matters during the window when it's needed most, and that's exactly when it blinds the model.

**Final recommendation: system-level blocklist (upstream deny-list) + single-stage mp_raw (no per-account gate).** The two layers are fully decoupled and each evaluated on the population it actually serves:

| Layer | Mechanism | Population | Performance |
|-------|-----------|------------|-------------|
| Blocklist | Cross-account deny-list | Post-lag fleet (61% of fleet accounts) | Precision=1.0 by construction |
| mp_raw | Per-account cosine distance, no gate | Cold-start fleet + all spoof types | Fleet residual PR=0.948; spoof k=1 PR=0.892 |

mp_raw wins across all three attack modes: spoof (cosine distance detects country/device shift), novel device (unseen tuple → high distance), and cold-start fleet (anomalous relative to home centroid despite being technically "known"). No gate, no rank-norm, no two-stage.

**Blocklist model assumptions (explicit):**

1. **Population trigger:** The blocklist is populated from *external* signals — customer complaints after an attack is observed. Training logins with the fleet device are normal usage and never trigger a complaint. The clock starts at the first *attack* event, not the first time the device appeared in training.
2. **Hard lag cutoff:** The experiment models a single fixed lag (BLOCKLIST_LAG_DAYS=10) — a uniform delay from first attack reported to blocklist activation. Real back-office pipelines have variable lag distributions; this is a point estimate of 3-5 days reporting + 3-5 days processing.
3. **Single triggering event:** The *earliest* fleet attack across all fleet accounts triggers the complaint. All subsequent fleet attacks from other accounts within the lag window are in the cold-start blind spot. After t_blocklist, all fleet accounts are caught regardless of their individual attack time.
4. **No false positives from blocklist:** The deny-list contains only confirmed fleet device keys. The experiment does not model blocklist errors (false additions or delayed removals).
5. **No feedback loop:** The model's own detections do not feed back into the blocklist. In a real system, mp_raw flagging a cold-start event could accelerate blocklist population — this would shrink the cold-start window and improve the composite architecture's coverage beyond what this experiment measures.

### 6.3 Rank-Normalization Tradeoff

Rank-norm improves cross-account score calibration and was motivated by the H2-RBA finding that it lifted overall spoof AUC. However, under heavy class imbalance it catastrophically degrades PR-AUC. Rank-normalization may be appropriate for balanced evaluation (equal spoof/benign), but is counterproductive for operational use where imbalance is the norm.

## 7. Limitations

1. **Synthetic accounts**: Accounts are generated from marginals, not real user histories. Genuine within-account temporal drift, session clustering, and multi-device correlations are not captured.

2. **Fleet attack model**: The blocklist temporal model rests on five explicit assumptions (see §6.2): (1) external complaint trigger — training logins with the fleet device do not start the clock; (2) hard lag cutoff at t_first + 10d, approximating a variable real-world distribution; (3) single triggering event — earliest attack across all fleet accounts activates the blocklist for all subsequent accounts; (4) no blocklist false positives; (5) no model-to-blocklist feedback loop. A real system where mp_raw detections accelerate blocklist population would reduce the cold-start window beyond what this experiment measures.

3. **Bootstrap sampling**: Bootstrap CIs are computed over all accounts pooled; within-account correlation is not modeled. Per-account calibration of thresholds is not evaluated.

4. **No threshold calibration**: Top-1% precision/recall uses a global score threshold. A production system would require per-account or per-risk-tier thresholds with calibration data.

---

## 8. Conclusions

1. **H6 CONFIRMED** (pre-registered hypothesis): Two-stage FastText mean-pool embedding beats trivial set-membership on k=1 country-change spoofing with non-overlapping 95% CIs (Δ+0.037 ROC-AUC). Note: hypothesis confirmation ≠ deployment endorsement — single-stage mp_raw dominates two-stage on every metric (see §6.2); the two-stage architecture is retired as a recommendation.

2. **PR-AUC is the only operationally honest metric**: mp_raw achieves 8.6× PR-AUC lift over trivial (0.892 vs 0.104) at 1:100 imbalance. ROC-AUC differences are misleadingly small.

3. **Rank-normalization is harmful under imbalance**: PR-AUC drops from 0.892 to 0.215 with rank-norm. Do not use rank-norm in operational scoring at realistic imbalance ratios.

4. **Fleet detection is a two-layer composite**: Blocklist catches 61% of fleet accounts (post-lag) with certainty; mp_raw handles the residual 39% cold-start window at PR=0.948 / 91.8% precision. Evaluating both layers together (combined scorer) produces a ranking artifact — PR drops to 0.771 — because post-lag events forced to score=1.0 disrupt the natural cosine-distance ordering. The residual view (pre-lag accounts only) is the honest metric for the model layer.

5. **k=1 failure was vocabulary poverty, not a fundamental limit**: H5 failed at k=1 on 30-token synthetic. H6 succeeds with 229-token RBA-calibrated vocabulary. Richer marginals resolve the embedding signal.
